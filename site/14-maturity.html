What works today, what works with limits, what is experimental

An honest map for October 2026, based on the documented workflows (page 11), benchmarks and vendor documentation. Use it to set expectations before you build.

Reliable to automate today

  • Script drafting from sourced research
  • Code-driven 2D animation, charts, kinetic type, math
  • Headless rendering (web-based frameworks, Manim, Blender)
  • Timeline assembly, transitions, compositing of overlays
  • TTS narration with word timestamps
  • Captions from timestamps; ASR-based caption QA
  • Loudness normalisation, ducking, encoding to platform specs
  • Silence/filler cutting of recorded talks
  • Scripted screen-capture product demos
  • Avatar presenter videos via platform APIs (with consent)
  • Objective QA: probes, detectors, OCR, timing checks
  • Partial re-rendering with a build graph

Works, with significant limits

  • Generative b-roll and cinematic inserts (needs takes + selection)
  • Image-to-video from designed keyframes
  • Character/product consistency across shots (references, LoRAs)
  • Agent judging visual quality from sampled frames
  • Whole-video review by a video-capable model (≈1 fps)
  • Editing real footage by conversation (with plan approval)
  • 3D scene building by agents (assets, lighting are weak spots)
  • Music generation with clear commercial rights
  • Lip-sync / dubbing for localisation
  • Multi-agent role decomposition (planner + coder + critic)

Experimental / not dependable

  • Fully autonomous cinematic short films from one prompt
  • Long continuous takes with stable identity and physics
  • AI judgement of temporal order, fine motion, physics plausibility
  • Agent "taste" over a whole film without human curation
  • Peer multi-agent "film crews" outperforming one strong agent
  • Real-time / interactive world models as virtual sets
  • CG-to-photoreal restyle without shimmer on long shots
  • Robust provenance that survives every re-encode

Inference — a synthesis. Supporting evidence: TOMATO / Vinoground on temporal reasoning; Code2Video and TheoremExplainAgent success rates; FilmAgent's small evaluation; video-use and product-launch-video workflows; C2PA survival tests.

Answers to the big questions

How close are today's coding agents to being autonomous directors, editors and production teams?

For code-driven and assembly-driven videos (explainers, product demos, presentations, edits of footage) they are already competent editors and motion designers with a supervisor: they plan, build, render, inspect and fix, and documented workflows exist. For cinematic, character-driven work they are production assistants: they run the machinery and pre-filter takes, but selection, taste and continuity remain human. Inference

What limitations prevent completely autonomous professional production?
  1. Perception: agents see samples of the video, not the video; temporal reasoning of current models is far below humans on benchmarks.
  2. Generative control: video models don't take exact specifications; consistency and physics are approximate.
  3. Taste and audience judgement: no reliable automatic measure of "good" for a specific audience.
  4. Cost of iteration: each generative attempt costs money; loops need budgets and stopping rules.
  5. Rights and responsibility: licences, consent and disclosure need an accountable human.
  6. Tool churn: models and APIs change or disappear within months (e.g. Sora's shutdown).
What would change this picture?
  • Coding agents with native, high-frame-rate video understanding and better temporal reasoning.
  • Generative models that accept exact 3D/camera/layout specifications (bridging A and B).
  • Stable, versioned model endpoints with reproducible seeds.
  • Evaluation suites tied to briefs (not generic aesthetics) for agent self-QA.
