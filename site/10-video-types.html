Twelve video types, twelve strategies

A stack that is perfect for a math explainer is useless for a music video. This page shows what makes each category technically different, what to build it with, how far an agent can automate it, and how quality is judged.

Category by category

1 · Educational and explanatory animation
What's different
Correctness and clarity beat realism. Viewers must follow one idea at a time; text, symbols and diagrams must be exact.
Methods
Code-driven 2D (math/diagram animation), narration-first timing, occasional stills or generated b-roll for context.
Tools (classes)
Manim-style Python libraries; React/HTML video frameworks; TTS + forced alignment; FFmpeg.
Audio
Narration is the backbone; light music bed; minimal SFX.
Automation
High — the strongest documented agent results are here (Code2Video, TheoremExplainAgent, Remotion/HyperFrames skills).
Limits
Layout overlaps and code errors were the top failures in TheoremExplainAgent; pedagogical quality still needs a teacher's eye.
Evaluate by
Accuracy, legibility, pacing; Code2Video's "TeachQuiz" idea — can a viewer answer questions after watching? Verified concept (arXiv 2510.01174)
2 · Scientific and engineering visualisation
What's different
The picture must be a function of real data or physics. A plausible-looking but wrong plume or trajectory is a defect, not a style.
Methods
Simulation → 3D render (Blender, ParaView) or 2D plots; annotation overlays; generative video only for non-technical context.
Automation
High for building and rendering; domain review required for correctness.
Evaluate by
Expert review of each claim; data provenance; units and scales.
3 · Professional business presentations
What's different
Brand compliance (fonts, colours, logos), data charts, speaker or narration, often generated from existing slides or documents.
Methods
Template-driven 2D (slides → animated scenes), charts from data, TTS or avatar presenter, captions.
Automation
High. Main risk: generic look; fix with a strong brand kit.
Evaluate by
Brand checklist, data accuracy, message clarity, length.
4 · Cinematic and photorealistic video
What's different
Lighting, lens, performance, continuity and emotion. Every frame is judged as a picture.
Methods
Keyframe-first: designed stills with references → image-to-video per shot → many takes → human selection → editing, grading, sound design. Professional AI films are heavily curated.
Automation
Low–medium. Agents can run the pipeline, generate candidates and pre-filter; selection and taste remain human.
Limits
Character consistency across shots, hands/physics, clip length; cost of many takes.
Evaluate by
Human review; continuity sheet; reference-identity checks.
5 · Marketing and advertising
What's different
Exact product appearance, legal claims, brand voice, many variants (formats, languages, A/B versions).
Methods
Product renders or photos (exact) + generated environments; templated variants; avatar or VO localisation.
Automation
Medium — variant generation is very automatable; the hero concept is not.
Limits
Generative models alter logos and product details; commercial-use terms of each tool; disclosure rules.
Evaluate by
Brand/legal review, product-fidelity checks, platform specs, performance metrics after launch.
6 · Social media content
What's different
Vertical 9:16, first 1–2 seconds decide retention, burned-in captions, fast pacing, trends.
Methods
Template assembly (stock/generated clips + TTS + big captions), clipping long videos, short generated clips with native audio.
Automation
High for templated formats (e.g. MoneyPrinterTurbo-style pipelines); originality is the bottleneck.
Evaluate by
Hook strength, caption legibility on a phone, safe areas, platform labelling rules for AI content.
7 · Documentary-style productions
What's different
Credibility: real footage, sourced facts, restraint. Generated imagery that looks like evidence can mislead.
Methods
Archive footage and stills (with motion), interviews, rendered reconstructions, generated atmosphere clearly as illustration; narration and score.
Automation
Medium: research and assembly yes; archive licensing and editorial ethics need humans.
Evaluate by
Fact audit, rights audit, disclosure of reconstructions.
8 · Animated storytelling
What's different
Characters that must stay on-model across dozens of shots, acting, dialogue, story structure.
Methods
Generative: character sheets + references/LoRAs → keyframes → I2V; or 3D characters with mocap; or 2D rigs. Multi-agent research systems (MovieAgent, ViMax, FilmAgent) target this.
Automation
Low–medium; research-grade systems exist, but evidence of consistent quality at length is thin.
Evaluate by
Character consistency checks, story review, audience test.
9 · Digital presenters and AI avatars
What's different
A single talking person; lip-sync and natural gestures; multilingual versions.
Methods
Avatar platforms (script → video) or open audio-driven models; slides/b-roll composited around the presenter.
Automation
High via platform APIs.
Limits
Uncanny moments over long takes; consent and likeness rules (vendors require on-camera consent for personal twins).
Evaluate by
Lip-sync quality (sync metrics, human check), pronunciation, viewer trust.
10 · Music videos
What's different
The song is fixed and is the timeline; visuals follow beats, sections and lyrics.
Methods
Beat/section analysis → shot plan → generated or rendered visuals → beat-synced cuts; research system AutoMV automates this end-to-end.
Automation
Medium: sync is mechanical; aesthetic coherence is curated.
Evaluate by
Sync accuracy, visual coherence, artist approval.
11 · Product demonstrations
What's different
Must show the real product truthfully; UI changes often.
Methods
Scripted browser/app automation recorded headlessly, zoom/cursor highlights, narration, captions; rendered product shots for hardware.
Automation
High for software: re-record automatically when the UI changes. Product-launch-video (EveryInc) is a documented Claude Code workflow for this. Verified
Evaluate by
Accuracy vs current product, readability of UI text, length.
12 · Hybrid productions mixing styles
What's different
Consistency across fundamentally different sources.
Methods
Strong style bible, one grade, shared typography, sound glue; deliberate style transitions.
Automation
Medium: routing and assembly automate; cohesion needs taste.
Evaluate by
Does it feel like one film? Human review.

Why one stack fails for another category

  • A Remotion/Manim stack is ideal for explainers and product demos but cannot produce believable people or landscapes.
  • A generative-video stack makes stunning cinematic shots but is the wrong tool for anything that must be exactly right (data, UI, math, physics, logos).
  • An avatar platform is unbeatable for a talking presenter and unusable for anything else.
  • A template video API scales social variants cheaply but gives generic results for storytelling.
  • A 3D engine gives total control but turns every new object into an asset-creation project.

End-to-end workflows (Deliverable F)

  1. Agent drafts outline + script from sources; you approve.
  2. TTS per segment → word timestamps.
  3. Agent writes scenes in a code framework (Manim or Remotion/HyperFrames), each scene's duration from its segment.
  4. Render previews; agent inspects stills at cue points and fixes overlaps.
  5. Music bed, ducking, loudness; captions from timestamps.
  6. Final render; ASR check; you review.
