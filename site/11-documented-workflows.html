The headline finding

Coding-agent video production that you can inspect today is overwhelmingly programmatic video (HTML/React/Manim/Blender-Python rendered to MP4) plus orchestration of generation APIs. It works well for motion graphics, explainers, product videos and edits of real footage. Evidence for fully autonomous cinematic generative films is research-grade (small human evaluations) or promotional. Almost every serious workflow keeps a human approval step. What the documented workflows have in common

  1. Code as the backbone: the agent writes something deterministic (HTML, React, Manim, Blender Python, FFmpeg) rather than relying only on generation.
  2. Human approval of the plan before expensive work (video-use's hard rule; product-launch-video's brief).
  3. Self-inspection of rendered output, almost always as frames/filmstrips + waveforms + transcripts, not as video.
  4. Parallel specialists (animation sub-agents, critic agents) under one coordinator.
  5. Hard-won rules written into skills — e.g. video-use's "subtitles applied last", "never cut inside a word", "30 ms audio fades at every boundary". These are the kind of production-correctness knowledge that turns an agent into a competent editor. Verified
  6. State in files (project notes, EDL/timeline JSON, cached transcripts, verify folders) so work can resume.
