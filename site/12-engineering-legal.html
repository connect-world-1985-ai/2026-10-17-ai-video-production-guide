Engineering, cost, legal and provenance

The invisible half of autonomous video production: keeping a growing pile of media, jobs and decisions reproducible, affordable, auditable — and legally usable. Not legal advice; this page describes the landscape as of October 2026 for a reader working in the EU.

Part 1 — Engineering challenges

A minimal, maintainable project layout

project/
  brief.md            research/            script.md        lexicon.yml
  style/              (palette, fonts, LUT, reference images)
  shotlist.json       timeline.json        decisions.log    costs.csv
  src/                (scene code: 2D, 3D scripts, physics models)
  jobs/               (external job table: provider, id, status)
  assets/manifest.json  → media in object storage, named by sha256
  qa/                 (reports, sampled frames, contact sheets)
  out/                (previews, masters, captions)
  build.py            (DAG: rebuild only what changed)

Illustrative. The point is that the plan, the code and every decision are text files an agent (or a colleague) can read and resume.

Part 2 — Copyright of AI output

JurisdictionPosition (Oct 2026)
USAHuman authorship required. The US Supreme Court declined to hear Thaler v. Perlmutter on 2 March 2026, leaving the ruling that AI alone cannot be an author in place. The Copyright Office's Part 2 report (29 Jan 2025) allows protection for human selection, arrangement and modification of AI material, case by case. Practice (law-firm reports)
EUNo specific rule; the "author's own intellectual creation" standard makes purely AI-generated output unlikely to be protected; the threshold for AI-assisted work is unsettled. A pending CJEU case (C-250/25) concerns training and outputs. Practice
GermanyCourts have ruled on training, not output ownership: LAION (OLG Hamburg, Dec 2025) allowed scientific text-and-data mining for dataset building; GEMA v OpenAI (LG München I, 11 Nov 2025) treated memorised lyrics in a model as reproduction. Practice (secondary)

Implication: the protectable parts of your video are the human parts — script, selection, editing, compositing, your own footage and voice. Log them. Contracts still decide who may use outputs. Inference

Part 3 — Commercial-use terms of tools

  • Free tiers are where commercial use is most often forbidden: e.g. Luma Free/Lite (watermarked, non-commercial), ElevenLabs Free (no commercial licence; attribution required), Suno Free (non-commercial; subscribing later doesn't relicense old songs). Practice (vendor help pages via search)
  • Indemnities are an enterprise feature: Google and OpenAI announced generative-AI indemnities for named services and paid tiers; Adobe's Firefly indemnity appears tied to enterprise agreements. Whether video models (Veo, Sora while it existed) are covered could not be confirmed. Unverified — ask for written terms.
  • Save a dated copy of the terms you generated under, with your plan tier. Terms change often.

Part 5 — Voice and likeness

  • Germany/EU: right to one's own image and general personality rights apply to deepfakes and voice imitation; GDPR covers biometric data. Get signed, scoped consent for any real person. Unverified in this research (general knowledge) — confirm with a German legal source.
  • Vendor checks: ElevenLabs requires voice owners to verify professional clones; HeyGen requires an on-camera consent statement for digital twins; Synthesia puts consent obligations on the customer. Vendor checks are partial — keep your own releases. Practice
  • Elsewhere: Tennessee's ELVIS Act (2024) and California's AB 2602/1836 (2025) are in force; the US federal NO FAKES Act was reported stalled in October 2026; Denmark introduced a bill (8 Oct 2026) to give people copyright-like protection over face and voice, targeted for 2027. Practice (secondary)

Part 6 — Labelling obligations

EU AI Act, Article 50 — applies from 2 August 2026

Providers of generative AI systems must mark outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way. Deployers must disclose deepfakes (realistic content depicting real people, places or events). A Code of Practice on marking and labelling was published on 10 June 2026 and endorsed by the Commission. If you use vendor tools to make videos, you are typically a deployer; if you host or offer a model, you may be a provider. Verified (European Commission); role classification is a legal question.

Platforms: YouTube requires creators to disclose realistic altered or synthetic content (not needed for clearly unrealistic animation); TikTok reads C2PA Content Credentials to auto-label; Meta shows "AI info" labels. Check current help pages. Practice

For the rocket documentary: generated shots of a real vehicle type are not deepfakes of people, but do not present generated imagery of real, branded vehicles as real footage; label reconstructions. Inference

Part 8 — Music, stock and archive material

  • Royalty-free ≠ copyright-free: a licence with a scope. Music needs composition + master rights for sync. Check online/monetised use.
  • AI music & Content ID: a paid-plan permission doesn't prove copyright or exclusivity; don't register generated tracks in Content ID without clear rights.
  • NASA media is generally not copyrighted, but insignia/logos need permission and no endorsement may be implied. ESA has mixed terms (some CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO; commercial use otherwise needs authorisation). Company media (e.g. a launch provider's photo stream) — check the licence per item. Practice (agency guidelines via search)

Part 9 — What the pipeline should record for every asset

Provenance manifest checklist
  1. Asset id, SHA-256, creation time, pipeline run id, step.
  2. Origin: generated · stock · public-agency · own footage · commissioned.
  3. Tool: vendor, product, model + version, API/UI, job id, seed.
  4. Account: plan tier and date; terms URL + dated snapshot hash.
  5. Open weights: licence id + file hash, territory check, revenue-threshold check.
  6. Inputs: prompt, references (with their own licences), LoRAs / voice ids.
  7. Rights: commercial use yes/no/conditional (+why), attribution string, indemnity, restrictions.
  8. People: real person depicted/voiced? consent record id and scope.
  9. Third-party marks/logos/characters: clearance note.
  10. Human contribution log (supports copyrightability).
  11. Provenance/marking: C2PA on input? watermark present? final signing step.
  12. Disclosure: AI Act role, label required, platform flags set.
  13. Review: reviewer, date, re-check date.

Build rule: fail the build if licence, plan tier or consent record is missing for any asset in the final timeline. Inference (from the R6 research synthesis)
