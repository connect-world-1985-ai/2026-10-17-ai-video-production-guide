Reference · §14 — Research methodology and evidence standards

Sources and method

How this guide was researched, how claims are labelled, and where to find every source.

Method

  1. Independent discovery (2026-10-17): six parallel research passes covered neural video; image, avatar and enhancement; programmatic graphics and editing; audio; agent workflows and video QA; legal and provenance. Each produced a sourced fact sheet with access dates and an explicit "disagreements/uncertainties" section.
  2. Primary sources first: official docs, pricing pages, repositories (stars and licences read from the GitHub API on 2026-10-17), papers, licence files. Secondary sources (news, law-firm notes, aggregators) were used where no primary page was reachable and are labelled as such.
  3. Main-author verification of the most consequential claims, directly from primary sources: Gemini API video pricing; the Artificial Analysis text-to-video leaderboard; the Wan 2.1 architecture numbers; the Sora shutdown; DaVinci Resolve 21.1 scripting change; the HunyuanVideo territory clause; GitHub statistics of the agent tools; the video-use skill file.
  4. No invented numbers: every price, star count and benchmark carries a source; where sources conflicted, the conflict is stated.

Evidence labels

LabelMeaning
VerifiedChecked against a primary source (docs, repo, paper, licence, official announcement), or confirmed by several independent reputable outlets.
PracticeEvidence-supported industry practice or widely documented behaviour.
InferenceThe author's technical analysis or synthesis — reasoned, not sourced as fact.
UnverifiedReported or marketed but not independently confirmed; conflicting sources.
ExperimentalEmerging capability: research or demo grade.

Known limitations of this research

  • Leaderboards have no snapshot dates and change weekly; rankings were read on 2026-10-17.
  • Several prices come from aggregators or resellers (marked); vendor pages sometimes failed to render.
  • GitHub stars measure interest, not production use; mirror repositories (Blender, FFmpeg) understate popularity.
  • German personality-rights law was not researched with primary sources.
  • Commercial "agent modes" inside video products were assessed only from secondary material.
  • No hands-on benchmarks were run; all quality claims come from published evaluations or documentation.
